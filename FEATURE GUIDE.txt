ROUTER HOME - FEATURE GUIDE
===========================
What every part of the Home tab does, what runs in the background, and where to change
things. For installing, see READ ME FIRST.txt. For what's new, see CHANGELOG.txt.

Most settings are in CONTROL CENTER: the switches icon in the Home tab's top bar.
The same guide is built into the Home tab: click "About" in the top bar, next to System
(searchable).
"Alerts" means the Discord alerts sheet: Control Center > Settings > Discord alerts.

1. THE HOME TAB, CARD BY CARD
===============================================================================
The Home tab is the first item in the router's admin menu. It refreshes itself every
few seconds, so you never need to reload it (except after an update: press Ctrl+F5
on Windows, or Cmd+Shift+R on a Mac).

TOP BAR
  Router name, a status light ("All systems normal" in green, or "1 warning" /
  "1 problem" in orange/red; click it to jump to the alerts), how many VPN tunnels are
  connected (e.g. "VPN 2/3"), online status, the eye icon (privacy mode, see section
  6), the Control Center button and a clock. Green lights blink slowly. New-device and
  new-firmware notices don't count as problems.

PAGE SEARCH (the box under the top bar)
  Type anything (a device name, "tunnel 2", "temperature", "quad9"...) and a list
  shows every card and line on the page that matches. Click one, or use the arrow
  keys and Enter, to scroll there: the card gets a blue outline and the line is
  highlighted. Matching settings are listed as "Settings > ..." and open the
  Settings window. The last item searches the About guide instead. Press / anywhere on
  the page to jump to the box, Esc to clear it. Values hidden by privacy mode can't be
  found, because they're never on the page.

ALERT BANNER (very top, only when something needs attention)
  - Red = serious (internet down, VPN tunnel down, DNS not answering, overheating,
    an important security check failing). Orange = warning (packet loss, running hot,
    new device, new firmware, ...).
  - Each alert says how long it has been happening ("since 3:42 PM, 12 min").
  - Buttons:
      Fix button   One-click fixes, always with a confirmation first: Restart tunnel,
                   Renew connection, Fix DNS, Restore settings.
      Open ...     Jumps to the right admin page.
      Clear        Packet-loss alerts only: restarts the 10-minute loss check from
                   scratch (e.g. right after changing a VPN server). The alert only
                   comes back if new loss shows up.
      Dismiss      Hides it for a while (1 hour for red, 24 hours for orange).
      It's mine    New-device alerts only: marks the device as known, so the alert
                   goes away for good.
  - A green "Resolved" card briefly appears when a problem clears by itself.

GREETING
  A one-line health summary ("All good" / "Heads up: ..."), status chips, and the
  WEATHER widget (see section 2 > Weather).
  - The chips always stay on one line. On narrower windows they shrink a little, then
    switch to short labels ("Online", "VPN 3/3", "Secure", "Backups OK", "2 new").
    Hover a chip to see its full text.
  - With NAS backup set up, a backups chip: "Backups healthy" (green) = the newest
    backup is under 8 days old and passed its restore check; orange = overdue, not
    verified or paused; red = failed or damaged. Click it for the NAS backup settings.
  - On routers with Tailscale installed, a "Tailscale on / off" chip sits next to
    "Security OK". Click it to open GL's Tailscale page. Note: GL doesn't allow
    Tailscale while a router is in access-point mode (it says "can only be used in
    router mode"); reach an access point through the main router's Tailscale instead.
  - Easter egg: click the weather widget for a live weather radar (Ventusky), centered
    on your ZIP. Nothing loads until you click, and closing it (X, Esc or a click
    outside) removes it completely, so it uses no memory afterwards. Its "Weather
    settings" button opens the ZIP / touchscreen settings.

SYSTEM
  - Gauges: CPU, memory, temperature (°F), connections, fan speed on models with a
    fan, and DHCP pool: how many of the router's DHCP addresses are handed out (active
    leases inside the pool, e.g. 28 of 150; hover for the range). The DHCP gauge only
    appears when the router runs DHCP itself (not in access-point mode).
  - Zram gauge (next to Memory, only when zram is on): zram is memory the router
    compresses instead of running out of RAM. The gauge shows how full it is and how
    much is in use; hover for the compression ratio, the real RAM it takes and the peak
    since the last restart. Orange from 60% full, red from 85%. To turn zram on, install
    GL's "zram-swap" package (System > Plug-ins); it sets itself up at every boot.
  - Clicking the fan gauge opens the Fan panel:
      Auto     The router's own fan control. Set "Turn the fan on at ___ °F" to make
               it start sooner or later.
      Manual   A fixed speed from 0 to 100%. A failsafe switches back to Auto if the
               router reaches its warning temperature, and alerts you.
  - Details: per-core CPU load, model, firmware, uptime, storage, memory, MAC address
    (hidden until you click the eye), thermal throttling.
  - Hardware acceleration (MediaTek models): whether the router's network chip is
    speeding up traffic. Showing 0 is normal while everything goes through a VPN.
  - Bufferbloat grade (A+ to F): how much your connection slows down while it's busy.
    Measured by the speed test.
  - NAS backup: when the last backup ran and whether it worked.
  - Recent events: how many events the router has recorded ("No events" or e.g.
    "12 events"). Click it for a timeline of everything the router noticed or did:
    outages, VPN failovers and auto-fixes, new devices, unusual uploads, sign-ins,
    settings changes and more, newest first; click an event for details. Recorded even
    when Discord alerts are off, kept for 14 days by default (also through restarts;
    change it with "Keep events for": 1 to 30 days). Privacy mode shows only the type
    and time. The type chips at the top (e.g. "VPN 3", "High ping 1") show only those
    types; "Clear" then removes only those types from the past hour, day or week, or
    all time (asks first; can't be undone). Each event has "Mute … alerts for a while".

INTERNET
  Online/offline, live download and upload speed with graphs, your public IP and
  gateway (hidden until you click the eye), WAN DNS (the internet connection's DNS),
  LAN DNS (what your devices are told to use; "From main router" on an access point),
  link speed, data totals. The details sit in side-by-side pairs (Gateway | Secondary
  WAN, WAN DNS | LAN DNS, Received | Sent, Cable modem signal | Modem uptime).
  - WAN ping: Cloudflare (1.1.1.1), Google (8.8.8.8) and Quad9 (9.9.9.9) are pinged
    straight over your internet connection (not through the VPN) every 10 seconds.
    You get a graph (one line per server) that switches between "Last hour" (one point
    per minute, fills in right away) and "24 hours" (one point per 15 minutes), with a
    ms scale on the side and times along the bottom; hover it for exact values. A rare
    huge spike is cut off at the top (and noted) so it doesn't flatten the rest.
    Below the graph, for each server: the ping now,
    the average over 10 minutes / 1 hour / 24 hours, jitter (how much the ping jumps
    around), packet loss over 1 hour and 24 hours, and the 24-hour range. If all three
    servers get slow at the same time, the problem is your connection, not the servers.
    The 24-hour numbers fill in during the first day (a router restart starts them over).
  - DNS in plain words:
      * WAN DNS = the DNS servers set on the internet connection.
      * GL's DNS page (Network > DNS) = where the router's own DNS service sends
        lookups. It answers the router itself and any device that asks the router.
      * LAN DNS = the address devices are told to use when they join. If it points
        at something else (e.g. AdGuard on a NAS), devices skip the router's DNS and
        that server's own upstream setting decides where their lookups go.
      * GL's IPv6 page has its own "LAN DNS" for IPv6 devices (no effect if IPv6 is off).

VPN  (replaced by "Access Point" in access point mode)
  - Each tunnel: connected or not, server, data used, kill switch, and when it last
    "checked in" with the VPN server (marked Stale after 3 minutes).
  - Tunnels are labelled "Tunnel 1 (Provider)".
  - Under each tunnel, its last 7 days: uptime, average ping, packet loss, daytime vs
    evening ping and average nightly speed. The steadiest tunnel is tagged MOST
    RELIABLE. "Past servers this week" (click to open) lists servers or providers you
    used earlier in the week. (This used to be a separate "VPN Reliability" card.)
  - Each tunnel has an on/off switch (it asks first).
  - Click a tunnel to change its server: a list of the servers saved in GL's VPN page,
    grouped by provider, with the current one ticked. A server another tunnel is using
    is greyed out. Switching asks first; the tunnel reconnects in a few seconds. Add
    new servers in GL's VPN page. Works for WireGuard and OpenVPN tunnels; each lists
    servers of its own type (changing a tunnel from WireGuard to OpenVPN or back is
    done in GL's VPN page).
  - ACCESS POINT card (when the router is an access point behind another router):
    the main router's address, internet latency, uplink and Wi-Fi clients.

VPN HEALTH  (next to VPN; hidden in access point mode)
  A plain checklist for each WireGuard or OpenVPN tunnel, with a green tick, orange !
  or red X:
  - Pings: Cloudflare and Quad9 through the tunnel (each shown). The router keeps one
    steady ping per server running, one a second, like "ping -t" on a PC, and sums it
    up every 10 seconds. Starting fresh pings every check made some VPN servers (Nord,
    Surfshark) pause their answers for ~30 seconds, which looked like stalls even
    though your devices were fine.
  - Domain lookup: example.com looked up through the tunnel, once a minute.
  - Web page check: an HTTPS page loaded through the tunnel, once a minute.
  - Handshake: when the tunnel last checked in with the VPN server. OpenVPN has no
    handshake, so its line is "Server responding": when data last came back from the
    VPN server ("Server gone quiet" after 3 minutes of nothing).
  - Drops: how many times it dropped (30+ seconds with no pings AND no web page) in the
    last hour and 24 hours, and when the last one was. If it's down right now, which
    tunnel its devices moved to.
  - Ping-only blips: times the pings stopped but web pages still loaded. These are
    ignored (no alert, devices aren't moved, not counted in the 7-day reliability
    numbers) and listed on their own line.
  - "Monitor 5 h" button (top right of the card): a closer 5-hour watch of every
    tunnel. Every ~3 seconds it reads the steady Cloudflare and Quad9 pings of each
    tunnel; every ~30 seconds (and during any stall) it also does a domain lookup and
    loads a web page through it. A stall only counts if web pages failed too (otherwise
    it's listed as a ping-only blip). Read-only. When it finishes you get ONE Discord
    summary: per tunnel, stalls with times and lengths, average ping, DNS and web
    results, and how many real drops vs ping-only blips the Home tab saw. The button
    counts down while it runs; click it to stop early (the summary is still sent).
    Main routers only (not in access point mode). A router restart stops it.
  - "Outage log" (bottom of the card): every REAL VPN outage (30+ seconds with no pings
    AND no web page through the tunnel) of the last 7, 30 or 60 days: when, how long,
    why, where its devices went and whether auto-heal restarted it. Pick a provider,
    then "Copy as text" or "Send to Discord" to paste into a support ticket. Ping-only
    blips aren't listed. Kept on the router for 60 days.
  - "Clear all VPN alerts" (bottom of the card): clears every VPN warning, moves any
    devices back to their own tunnels and resets the drop history. Tunnels are then
    re-checked from scratch; a tunnel that really is down shows up again in ~30 s.
  - Changing tunnels is safe: turning one off or removing it never sends an alert, a
    tunnel switched to another server gets 2 minutes to reconnect and starts with a
    clean history, and new tunnels are picked up right away.

WI-FI UPLINK  (travel routers, or any router using Wi-Fi as its internet)
  Sits next to Internet; the VPN card moves further down.
  - Which Wi-Fi network the router is linked to, signal strength (bars, dBm and
    Excellent / Good / Fair / Weak), band and channel, Wi-Fi generation, security,
    link speed, retry rate, IP / gateway / DNS, the router's MAC on that network
    (hidden until you click the eye), MAC type and how long it's been connected.
  - Choose network: your saved networks (connect with one click, no password), nearby
    networks from a scan (about 20 seconds; strongest first, with band and security),
    or "Join a hidden network". While it switches (up to a minute) the router's own
    Wi-Fi stays up but internet pauses. Business (802.1X) networks: use GL's Internet
    page. Networks the router has used before show their report-card grade.

TRAVEL ROUTERS  (Slate, Beryl, etc., or any router getting internet over Wi-Fi / a phone)
  These only read and show things; nothing changes your settings.
  - HOTEL SIGN-IN PAGE: hotels and airports often need you to accept a login page
    first, and devices behind a travel router never see it. The router checks every
    minute. When there is one, an orange banner "This Wi-Fi needs you to sign in" has
    an "Open sign-in page" button. Accept it and the router goes online by itself.
  - THIS NETWORK card: a safety check each time you join a network (or press Check
    again): encrypted or open Wi-Fi, router hidden from other guests, VPN on or off,
    who answers your DNS lookups, where websites think you are, Tailscale, and the VPN
    leak test when tunnels are on. Verdict: Looks safe / Mostly safe / Be careful.
    Also data used today and in total on this network, and the Home check.
  - HOME CHECK: every 5 minutes it pings your home router through Tailscale (direct or
    relayed, and the ping). If home doesn't answer for 15 minutes you get a
    "Can't reach home" banner and message.
  - NETWORKS YOU'VE USED: a report card per network: grade A-F from ping, packet loss,
    drops and speed, signal, data used, security, last used, and what it's good for
    (video calls, HD or 4K streaming). "Test this network" runs a speed test to fill in
    the speed. Forget removes a card (not the saved Wi-Fi).
  - DATA LIMIT (This Network > Settings): optional GB per network per day, with a
    warning at 80% and 100% (paid hotel Wi-Fi, phone hotspot).

WI-FI  (models with Wi-Fi)
  Each band (2.4 / 5 / 6 GHz): network name, channel, channel width, number of
  connected devices, and Wi-Fi 7 multi-link (MLO) devices.

ADGUARD HOME
  - Whether AdGuard ad blocking is on, and which DNS server your devices actually use.
  - If AdGuard runs on a NAS or other computer instead, connect it with this card's
    "Connect AdGuard on my NAS" button and the card shows "Running on your NAS" with live stats: protection on/off,
    queries, % blocked, response time, a 24-hour graph and the top blocked domains.

CLIENTS
  - Every connected device. NEW = joined in the last 24 hours. PAUSED = internet paused.
  - Pause button: cuts that device's internet right away (and its open connections).
    The resume button turns it back on. You're asked before pausing the device that
    provides DNS for your whole network.
  - Click a device to rename it, see its details, or block/unblock it.

NETWORK HEALTH
  Ping time and packet loss for your internet and through each VPN tunnel (now and over
  the last 10 minutes), plus whether your DNS server is answering and how fast. Each
  tunnel is tested against two servers at once (Cloudflare and Quad9) and only counts
  as down when both get no answer.
  Internet health, at the top of the card:
  - Verdict: Internet Good / Fair / Poor for the last hour, with the reason (e.g. "35%
    lag spikes · jitter 92 ms").
  - Where a problem is: Router -> Modem -> Internet, with the ping to each. If the modem
    answers fine but the internet doesn't, it says the trouble is on your ISP's side.
    (On an access point the middle step is the main router.)
  - A warning if your modem has gone into router mode (double NAT: this router gets a
    private address like 10.x from it), or if your ISP uses carrier-grade NAT.
  - Jitter and lag spikes on the Direct (ISP) tile. Spikes = checks over your high-ping
    limit (Discord alert settings, default 75 ms).
  - Good for: Browsing / Video calls / Gaming, each ✓, ! or ✗, from the last hour.
  - Lag spikes, jitter and no-answer % for the last hour; online % today and over 7 days
    with the last outage (and its likely cause); a 24-hour strip with one colored block
    per hour (hover for the details); and your cable modem signal (if the modem reader
    is set up).
  - More rows below: the busiest device right now (and its share of the traffic), DNS
    (how fast your DNS answers vs usual, new names, AdGuard's blocked share), ping under
    load (measured whenever the line is busy, e.g. a big download; grade A+ to F),
    connections (how full the router's connection table is) and IPv6 (off / on, and
    whether it could bypass the VPN).
  - Each VPN tunnel row has a Good / Fair / Poor grade for the last 10 minutes.

SECURITY
  - 11 live checks, re-run every minute: SSH login type, admin page HTTPS, VPN key file
    permissions, unused firewall openings, remote access, UPnP, disabled services, etc.
  - "Firmware-upgrade safe": whether your Home tab and settings will survive a
    firmware upgrade.
  - Whether Discord alerts are working.

SENSORS            Every temperature sensor in °F.
LAST 24 HOURS      Temperature and internet traffic charts. Kept across reboots.
SERVICES           On/off state of every router service.

GL.INET UTILITIES TOOLKIT  (the last card on the page)
  Point-and-click tools inspired by phantasm22's GL.iNet Utilities Toolkit
  (github.com/phantasm22/GL-iNet_utils), rebuilt from scratch for Router Home.
  - Hardware: CPU type and clock, memory, storage, temperatures (°F), network ports,
    Wi-Fi radios (band, channel, width, power), and a VPN crypto check: whether AES
    instructions, NEON, a hardware crypto engine and fast ChaCha20 (WireGuard's cipher)
    are active.
  - Benchmarks (one at a time, with a Stop button; results stay until the router
    restarts):
      CPU thermal stress: all cores at full load for 30 s, 1 or 2 min; shows the peak
        temperature and whether the CPU slowed itself down. Stops by itself at 203 °F.
      VPN & crypto speed: ChaCha20, AES-128-GCM and AES-256-GCM on one core, plus RSA.
      Storage speed: writes 64 MB to the flash, reads it back, deletes it.
      Memory speed: RAM and the RAM disk.
      DNS latency: 10 popular sites at your devices' DNS, the router's DNS, Cloudflare,
        Quad9 and Google.
  - Zram & system: change zram's size and compression (zram restarts in a few seconds;
    "Back to defaults" undoes it), and check the package system (remove a leftover lock
    from an unfinished install, refresh the package lists).
  - SSH keys: see, add and remove the keys that can sign in as root. The last key can't
    be removed while password sign-in is off, so SSH can't be locked out by accident.

TOP DEVICES BY DATA      Biggest users over 24 hours and 7 days.

SPEED TEST
  - Uses Ookla's Speedtest (only if you accepted Ookla's terms during install).
  - Buttons: Test Internet (no VPN), Test VPN, Test Both (Internet + every tunnel).
    Test VPN asks which tunnel: pick one (each shows its last result), or "All
    tunnels" to test them one after another. Testing one tunnel only updates its own
    row; the other tunnels keep their earlier results, marked with how old they are.
  - Shows ping, jitter, packet loss, download, upload and a bufferbloat grade.
  - Runs automatically every night (default 4 AM). A full nightly "Test Both" can use
    several GB on fast connections. Turn it off or change the time in Alerts.
  - ISP button: the ISP report card (see section 2).
  - VPN & DNS LEAK TEST (main routers), its own box in the card (the card is called
    "Speed Test & Leak Test"): "Run leak test" checks, in about 30 seconds and without
    changing or interrupting anything, that each tunnel hides your home address, devices
    on the VPN can only leave through a tunnel, the kill switch is armed, IPv6 can't
    bypass the VPN and DNS lookups go through it. The DNS part also works with the VPN
    off: it names the company that answers your lookups (Quad9, Cloudflare, Google,
    OpenDNS... or your ISP, flagged as a leak).
  - AUTOMATIC LEAK TEST: main routers also run the full leak test by themselves every
    hour, in the background. If a check fails you get a "Possible VPN / DNS leak" alert
    naming what failed, and a "Leak test is clear again" message once it passes. Both
    appear in Recent events. Skipped in access-point mode and while the internet is down.
  - HOURLY DNS CHECK: the box's top line shows the DNS provider found by the hourly
    check, since when, and a short history. A message is sent if your ISP starts
    answering your lookups, or a provider you've never used appears.

WHO'S HOME  (hidden by default: Control Center > Settings > Layout > Edit > Show)
  Shows who's home and since when. To add someone, click their phone in the Clients
  card and fill in "This is ___'s phone". Home = phone connected; away = not seen for
  10 minutes (phones nap). Optional Discord messages on arrive/leave.

DATA THIS MONTH  (main router only)
  Internet usage for your billing month: total, download/upload, a bar per day and an
  "on pace for" estimate. Click "Set a data cap" to enter your cap in GB (or tick
  Unlimited to just see usage) and the day your billing month starts. Discord warns at
  80% and 100%. It's measured at the router, so your ISP's meter can differ a little.

SPEED HISTORY
  Your speed tests over the last 60 days: one line for the internet and one per VPN
  tunnel. Switch between Download and Upload.

ADS BLOCKED BY DEVICE  (AdGuard on this router, or on a NAS that is connected)
  Which devices AdGuard blocked the most, from its last 500 blocked lookups. Needs
  AdGuard's query log turned on (AdGuard > Settings > General settings).
  - If AdGuard Home runs ON this router, it's used first. Your browser reads its log
    through the admin page, with the login you're already signed in with.
  - GL's built-in AdGuard usually sees every lookup as coming from the router itself.
    In that case the card shows the most-blocked SITES instead of devices.

DEVICE SHEET EXTRAS  (click any device in the Clients card)
  Internet schedule  Pause that device's internet on chosen days between two times
                     (e.g. 22:00-07:00 on school nights). Overnight times are fine.
                     Best set on the MAIN router, which controls everyone's internet.
  Who's home         "This is ___'s phone" adds the person to Who's Home.
  Wake-on-LAN        Adds a Wake button for this computer in Control Center. The
                     computer must have Wake-on-LAN enabled and be wired.

DOCK (bottom)      Shortcuts to the main admin pages, plus a red "DNS" button at the end.

"IT'S ALWAYS DNS, HUB"  (easter egg: the red DNS button in the dock)
  Everything about DNS on your network in one window. Read-only.
  - Verdict: "It's not DNS (this time)" or "It's DNS. It's always DNS.", from the live
    DNS check, with how fast your DNS server just answered.
  - How a lookup travels: a complete map of where every lookup really ends up.
      * Your devices -> LAN DNS (e.g. AdGuard on your NAS) -> AdGuard's upstream (DoH/
        DoT shown as encrypted) -> who actually answers.
      * Devices that set their own DNS -> redirected to the router (if "override
        clients' DNS" is on) -> where the router sends lookups.
      * The router itself -> its DNS service -> GL Manual DNS (or AdGuard on the
        router, or the WAN DNS when automatic). WAN DNS shows as "not used" when the
        GL DNS page is on Manual.
      * Each VPN tunnel gets a box: its devices (if they ask AdGuard on the NAS
        directly, the tunnel's own DNS isn't used for them), and the router's lookups
        that follow the tunnel: the VPN's DNS, or crossed out with "Overridden by"
        and what replaced it.
  - Every DNS setting: WAN DNS, LAN DNS, the GL DNS page, the router's DNS service,
    "override VPN DNS", "override clients' DNS", rebind protection, IPv6 DNS,
    AdGuard on the router and on your NAS (DNSSEC, cache, today's lookups and blocked
    share), and DNS health over the last 10 minutes.
  - Who answers your devices' lookups, with the last 48 hourly checks.
  - DNS Race: type any domain and it is looked up at every DNS server at once
    (AdGuard, this router, Quad9, Cloudflare, Google, and each VPN tunnel's DNS
    through the tunnel). Fastest gets a medal, the slowest a turtle, failures say
    "It was DNS."
  - Lookup map (below the race): type a name like weather.com and see what it took
    to find it: your DNS (did AdGuard block it, answer from memory, or ask its
    upstream, e.g. Quad9 over DoH, and how long that took), then the full trail a
    resolver follows when it doesn't know the answer yet: a root server -> the .com
    servers -> the site's own servers -> the address, and how long it's remembered.
    (On an access point the full trail can't be followed, because the main router
    redirects DNS sent to outside servers; the map says so.)
  - The famous DNS haiku and a random DNS fact each time you open it.
  On an access point, the hub shows that DNS comes from the main router.


===============================================================================
2. CONTROL CENTER
===============================================================================
Switches     VPN tunnels on/off, AdGuard on/off, LEDs on/off (models with LEDs).
             Every change asks for confirmation.

Back up settings
             Downloads a full backup of the router's settings to your computer.

Restart router
             Asks first.

Restart modem
             Presses Restart on your cable gateway (asks first). Everyone is offline
             for about 5-10 minutes. Only shown in router mode, once the cable modem
             is connected (Settings > Cable modem). At most once every 10 minutes.

Wake ...     One row per computer you enabled Wake-on-LAN for; press Wake to turn it on.

Settings     Everything below opens in one SETTINGS window (Control Center > Settings
             > Open), grouped:
               Privacy & look     Privacy mode, Look, Accent color / Colors, Live
                                  wallpaper (colors slowly move; on by default), Layout
               Alerts & backups   Discord alerts, Auto-heal, NAS backup
               Internet           Cable modem (main routers), ISP report card
               Home               Weather
             Look and color changes apply right away and the window stays open; the
             other rows open their own settings window.
             Search: type in the box at the top (e.g. "color", "backup", "xfinity",
             "screenshot") to show only matching settings; Esc clears it. The page
             search bar finds settings too ("Settings > Cable modem"); pick one to open
             the window right on it.

Privacy mode
             The eye icon (see section 6).

Look         macOS or Android. "Android" switches the whole Home tab to a colorful
             Android 17-style design: every card in its own color, big rounded cards,
             pill buttons, Android-style switches, vivid playful icons and a Quick-
             Settings-style Control Center.

Layout       Edit: move cards (◀ ▶), resize them (S / M / L) or hide them; Done when
             finished, Reset to undo everything. Saved per browser.

Accent color (macOS look only) Like macOS's Appearance settings: Multicolor, Blue,
             Purple, Pink, Red, Orange, Yellow, Green or Graphite. Buttons, switches,
             links and the background tint change to match. Saved per browser.

Colors       (Android look only) Pick a color theme, like Android's "Material You":
             Playful (multi-color), Ocean, Forest, Sunset, Grape, Rose, Citrus, Mint
             or Slate. Cards, buttons, switches, icons and the background all change
             to match. Saved per browser. It follows the router
             page's light/dark mode. The choice is saved in that browser only, so each
             computer or phone can use its own.

Alerts       Discord alerts: webhook, alert types, schedules (see section 3).

Auto-heal    The switches for everything the router fixes by itself (see section 4).

NAS backup
             Backs up the router's settings to your NAS on a schedule (default
             Sunday 4 AM).
             Methods: FTP, FTPS (encrypted, port 21 or 990), WebDAV, or SSH/SCP with a
             key the router creates for you.
             Keeps the newest N backups and deletes older ones. "Save & back up now"
             tests it. Error messages say what's wrong in plain words (FTP turned off
             on the NAS, encryption required, wrong password, ...). The password is
             stored on the router only and is never shown again.

AdGuard on my NAS
             (On the AdGuard card, not in Settings.) Connects the AdGuard card to an
             AdGuard Home running elsewhere (address with port, plus its login).

Weather      Enter a US ZIP code. Weather comes from Open-Meteo (free, no account).
             On models with a touchscreen, the forecast can also appear on the screen's
             sleep display.
             Radar background (touchscreen models): a live radar map behind the weather
             on the sleep display, refreshed every 15 minutes (Iowa State University /
             National Weather Service). With no rain nearby it shows a sun, or a moon
             and stars at night. Switch it off here to get the plain background back.

ISP report   Monthly internet uptime and outages, average and slowest speeds, and how
             often tests reached 80% of your plan. Set your plan speeds here. "Copy as
             text" gives you something to send your internet provider.

Fan          On models with a fan: opened by clicking the fan gauge on the System card.

3. DISCORD ALERTS
===============================================================================
Setting up
  1. In Discord: Server Settings > Integrations > Webhooks > New Webhook.
     Pick a channel, then copy the webhook URL.
  2. In the Home tab: Control Center > Settings > Discord alerts. Paste the URL, then
     press Save and Send test.
  The webhook is stored on the router only (readable by its admin account only).

Search box: the top of the Alerts window has a search box. Type a word ("vpn",
  "upload", "auto fix", "reboot", "weekly"...) and only the matching alert types,
  schedule settings and auto-fix switches stay visible. Settings you can't see while
  searching are kept as they are when you press Save.

You can turn each type on or off:
  VPN                A tunnel goes down or stale, devices moved to another tunnel and
                     back, auto-heal restarts.
  Internet outage    Sent when the connection is back, with how long it was down.
                     Down = no answer from Cloudflare, Quad9 or Google for about 30 s.
                     Not sent by routers in access-point mode (the main router
                     reports real outages); in router mode it is.
  High ping          Your internet ping (best of Cloudflare, Google and Quad9, measured
                     directly, not through the VPN) is over your limit (default 75 ms,
                     set it under Thresholds):
                       - high: the middle value of the last 5 minutes is over it, or
                       - spiky: 25% or more of the checks in the last 15 minutes were
                         over it (lag spikes even though most pings are fine).
                     One spike never counts. Built not to spam: at most one alert an
                     hour (if it keeps bouncing, the next message says how often), one
                     reminder if it's still bad after 2 hours, and one "back to normal"
                     after 10 calm minutes. Paused during speed tests and outages. Not
                     sent by access points. A banner shows on the Home page meanwhile.
  DNS                Your DNS server (e.g. AdGuard) stops answering, and when it's back.
                     Also: new lookups getting slow (over 250 ms for 10 minutes), saying
                     whether it's AdGuard / the NAS or the DNS provider itself (at most
                     every 3 hours; not while the whole line is lagging).
  Internet outage    now also says the likely cause: the modem kept answering = your
  (cause)            ISP's side; the modem didn't answer either = the modem or its cable.
  Modem mode         (Internet type) The modem slipped into router mode (double NAT),
                     with the steps to turn bridge mode back on; and when it's fixed.

PHONE VIEW
  The 📱 button in the menu bar switches to a compact page for phones: overall status,
  the internet grade, ping and speed, your VPN tunnels with on/off switches, devices
  online and the busiest one, and big buttons (speed test, test VPN, clear alerts,
  recent events, DNS hub, outage log, settings, full page). It turns on by itself on
  small screens; your choice is remembered in that browser.

OUTAGE & LAG LOG (for your ISP)
  ISP report card → "Outage & lag log (30 days)" (also "Log" in Network Health): every
  internet outage with its likely cause, lag episode, cable modem restart and slow speed
  test, plus a table per day (ping, evening ping, lag spikes, no answer). "Copy as text"
  or "Send to Discord". The per-day table fills in from 11-18 on.

MORE ALERTS
  Evenings          Every night at 11 PM: if the internet was much worse from 7 to 11 PM
  (ISP type)        on 4 of the last 7 days, one message (at most weekly). That usually
                    means your ISP's local network is overloaded at busy times.
  Modem restart     The cable modem's uptime started over: when, and how long it had
  (Internet type)   been up. Several a week is worth reporting to your ISP.
  Unusual blocking  Opt-in ("Watch for unusual blocking" under Thresholds): one device
                    suddenly has 5x its usual blocked lookups (300+ in an hour) → one
                    message with the domains it was trying to reach.

RECONNECT WHEN THE VPN SERVER IS BACK (auto-heal, on by default)
  After auto-heal has given up on a tunnel (or it has been down 30 minutes), the router
  keeps pinging that tunnel's VPN server. As soon as it answers again, the tunnel is
  reconnected (hourly tries if the server never answers pings; at most 6 a day).

MUTE, CLEAR ALL AND THE DAILY DIGEST
  - "Mute" on a Home page alert (or "Mute … alerts" on a Recent event): no banner and no
    Discord message for that type for 1 hour, the rest of today or a week. Still listed
    in Recent events. "N muted · manage" under the alerts (and the Discord alerts
    window) lists what's muted with Unmute buttons.
  - "Clear all" under the alerts: the VPN, packet-loss and high-ping checks start over
    from scratch (a real problem comes back within minutes); the rest are hidden like
    Dismiss.
  - Daily digest (Discord alerts window): tick less urgent types (new devices, settings
    changed, sign-ins, who's home, schedules, watched devices, Wi-Fi report, unusual
    uploads, speed tests, backups, router health, ISP speed, data cap, firmware) and they
    arrive together as one message a day at the time you pick.
  New device         A device joins your network for the first time.
  Security           A security check starts failing or passes again.
  Temperature        The router stays above your limit (default 185 °F) for 5+ minutes.
  Firmware           A new GL firmware is out, with a checklist of what an upgrade
                     would keep or lose.
  Upgrade report     After an upgrade: what survived and what needs attention.
  Speed              Nightly speed test results.
  Sign-ins           Admin page and SSH logins, and failed SSH attempts.
  Weekly report      A summary (default Sunday 9 AM): uptime, outages, speeds, VPN
                     reliability, top devices, backups.
  NAS backup         A scheduled backup failed.
  Router health      Automatic reboots, stuck services restarted, clock problems,
                     storage/memory nearly full, cable speed drops.
  Settings changed   Hourly list of router settings that changed. Passwords and keys
                     are never shown. LEDs turning on/off (e.g. an LED schedule) are
                     not reported.
  Slow internet      3 nightly speed tests in a row under 70% of your plan.
                     (Skipped on access points, whose own tests go through the main
                     router.)
  Wi-Fi report       The weekly Wi-Fi channel report.
  Schedules          A device was paused / is back online on its schedule.
  Who's home         Someone arrived or left.
  Data cap           80% and 100% of your monthly data cap.

If the internet is down, alerts are saved and sent once it's back.

4. THINGS THE ROUTER DOES BY ITSELF
===============================================================================
All of these can be switched on or off in Control Center > Settings > Auto-heal.
(They used to be inside the Discord alerts window.) Each fix still sends a Discord
message when it runs, as a VPN or Router health alert, and is listed in Recent events.
The weekly Wi-Fi report stays in Discord alerts, since it's a report, not a fix.

RESTART A TUNNEL STUCK "CONNECTING" (on by default)
  A tunnel that's switched on but hasn't connected for 10 minutes gets its connection
  process restarted, at most 3 times in 6 hours. Then you're told to try another server.

RESTART A SERVICE WHOSE MEMORY KEEPS GROWING (on by default)
  Checked hourly. If one service (web server, Tailscale, DNS, AdGuard) grows for 6 hours
  by 15 MB+ while the router is short on memory, just that service is restarted, at most
  once a day. Much gentler than a reboot.

TRY SQM FOR LAG UNDER LOAD (opt-in, two warnings first)
  If a speed test shows your ping rising 60+ ms while the line is busy, GL's SQM traffic
  shaping is turned on at 85% of your measured speed. The next speed test decides: it
  stays only if the lag drops a lot without losing more than half your speed; otherwise
  your previous SQM settings come back by themselves. SQM can lower your top speed, and
  it doesn't help lag spikes on an idle line (an ISP problem). Never touches SQM you
  turned on yourself; at most one try a week; turning the switch off undoes it.

FORGET REMOVED VPNs
  VPNs you remove from GL drop out of the speed history, 7-day reliability and last speed
  results by themselves 24 hours later (switch it off in the "Forget removed VPNs"
  dialog). "Forget removed VPNs" (Speed History card, or under "Past servers this week")
  shows exactly what will go and does it right away. By default the history of older
  servers from a provider you still use is kept (option in the dialog). The × next to a
  Speed History line or a past server removes just that one.

RESTART VPN TUNNELS THAT STAY DOWN (auto-heal)
  A tunnel (WireGuard or OpenVPN) that's still down after 2 minutes is disconnected and reconnected
  (its devices are already on a working tunnel by then, see failover below). If it's
  still down, it tries again every 10 minutes, at most 3 times per outage. If all 3
  tries fail you get one message saying which tunnel its devices are using meanwhile.
  It doesn't try while the internet itself is down.

HOW A TUNNEL COUNTS AS "DOWN"
  Every 10 seconds the router pings through each tunnel and checks that it is still
  talking to the VPN server. Once a minute it also loads a small secure web page
  through each tunnel. A tunnel counts as down if pings fail for ~30 seconds, OR if
  web pages fail twice in a row (~2 minutes) even though pings still work.

MOVE DEVICES TO A WORKING TUNNEL (VPN failover; needs 2 or more tunnels, WireGuard
or OpenVPN in any mix)
  If a tunnel stops working for about 30 seconds, the devices using it are moved into
  another working tunnel. If that one fails too, they move again to the next working
  tunnel. Once their own tunnel has been healthy for about a minute, they move back.
  - Devices never leave the VPN. If every tunnel is down, the kill switch blocks them,
    exactly as before.
  - Open connections (calls, games, downloads) reconnect once at each move.

REBOOT THE ROUTER IF IT HAS A MAJOR PROBLEM
  Only for serious problems that LAST, and only after a gentler fix didn't help:
    - memory under 5% free for 15 minutes (the admin services are restarted first),
    - connection table over 95% full for 15 minutes,
    - admin/API services still stuck 10 minutes after being restarted.
  It will NOT reboot:
    - more than once in 24 hours or twice in 7 days,
    - within 6 hours of the router starting,
    - during a speed test, NAS backup or firmware upgrade.
  Problems where the router still mostly works wait for 2-5 AM. You get a Discord
  message before ("Rebooting...: reason") and after ("Back after 1 min 10 s").
  Normal blips like a VPN drop, an internet outage or a slow speed test NEVER cause a
  reboot.

OTHER SELF-CARE
  Stuck admin services   Restarted if they stop answering for 3 minutes.
  Clock                  Checked every 30 min. If time sync fails, the time service is
                         restarted. A wrong clock breaks VPNs and secure websites.
  Capacity               Warns when the connection table, storage, RAM disk, memory or
                         the DHCP pool (90%) gets nearly full (once, then again only
                         after it recovers).
  Internet renew         Main routers: if the cable to the modem is connected but there
                         is no internet for 3 minutes, the connection to your ISP is
                         renewed once per outage (switch: "Renew the internet connection
                         if it drops"). If that doesn't help you're told it's probably
                         the modem or ISP.
  Router DNS             If the router's own DNS stops answering even for itself for 3
                         minutes, it is restarted (at most every 30 min). Never touches
                         AdGuard or its settings (switch: "Restart the router's DNS...").
  Tailscale              If Tailscale is switched on but not connected for 10 minutes,
                         it's restarted (at most once an hour). If it's signed out you
                         get a message instead (switch: "Restart Tailscale..."). It
                         waits while the internet itself is down.
  Unexpected restart     One message if the router started without shutting down
                         normally (usually a power cut, sometimes a crash).
  Backup restore check   After every NAS backup: the newest copy is downloaded back
                         into memory, checked to be complete and to contain the
                         router's settings, then deleted. Never changes the NAS.
  Unusual upload         Main routers, every 10 min: if a device uploads 1 GB+ in about
                         an hour AND 10x its normal (7-day average), you get one message
                         per device per day. Could be a backup, or a hacked device or
                         malware sending data out. A speed test on one of your own
                         GL.iNet routers behind this one doesn't count (it's one short
                         burst); steady uploading from them still does.
  Turned-off services    Every 10 min: if a service you turned off is running again
                         (e.g. after a firmware update), you get one message. With the
                         opt-in switch "Turn a service off again" it's stopped and
                         disabled again instead (left running if it comes straight back).
  NAS backup retry       A backup that failed because the NAS was asleep, rebooting or
                         busy is retried after 1 hour, then 6 hours. Not when the NAS is
                         full or the password/folder is wrong (you're told what to fix).
  Wake the NAS           When the NAS goes offline, or doesn't answer right before a
                         backup, it's sent a Wake-on-LAN signal. Only works if
                         Wake-on-LAN is turned on in the NAS's own settings.
  VPN leak fix           If the hourly leak test finds traffic leaving outside a tunnel,
                         a tunnel showing your home address, or the kill switch's
                         blocking rule missing, the VPN routing is re-applied (tunnels
                         reconnect for a few seconds) and the test runs again 2 minutes
                         later. Settings problems (IPv6, DNS) are reported, never changed.
  Connection table       Over 90% full for 5 minutes: the one device holding most of the
                         connections has them cleared (it reconnects), or idle
                         connections expire sooner until it's back under 60%.
  Storage clean-up       Storage or RAM disk nearly full: deletes files that are safe to
                         lose (package lists, leftover temporary files). Never settings,
                         history or undo backups.
  Access point link      Access points: cable connected but the main router hasn't
                         answered for 3 minutes -> renew the address; 3 minutes later ->
                         restart the network link once.
  Quieter channel        OPT-IN. Applies the weekly report's quieter channel at 3 AM and
                         switches back if devices don't reconnect within 7 minutes. Only
                         bands set to a fixed channel; never radar-check (DFS) channels.

CABLE MODEM RESTART (OPT-IN, LAST RESORT; main routers with the gateway password saved)
  Turning it on shows two warnings first (the second needs a tick box), because it's
  the only fix that changes another device. When the internet has been down for 10
  minutes and renewing the connection didn't help, the Home tab shows a red banner,
  "Restarting the cable modem in 5 min", with a Cancel restart button. At 15 minutes the
  router presses Restart on the Xfinity gateway (in bridge mode that button only
  restarts it; it is not a factory reset). At most once a day; never during a speed
  test or firmware upgrade. You're told when the internet is back, or after 20 minutes
  that it's probably an Xfinity outage. "Check (no restart)" in the Auto-heal window
  tests the login and finds the button without pressing it.
  Log flood              If the router logs 300+ lines a minute for 3 minutes (a service
                         stuck in an error loop), you get a message naming the source.
  Cable check            Watches the cable to your modem (or to the main router in
                         access point mode). Alerts if it runs slower than its best
                         speed (e.g. 1 Gbps instead of 2.5) or keeps disconnecting.
                         Usually a loose or damaged cable.
  Weekly Wi-Fi report    Models with Wi-Fi, Sunday 3 AM. Scans for neighbouring
                         networks (a brief, unnoticeable scan) and reports how crowded
                         each band's channel is. It only suggests a change when another
                         channel is clearly quieter. YOU decide whether to change it, in
                         the admin page > Wireless.

KEEPING YOUR DATA
  Charts and history are saved before a reboot and restored after it. Everything
  survives firmware upgrades when "Keep settings" is selected. After an upgrade, the
  Home tab and a Discord report tell you about anything the upgrade switched back on.

5. THE INSTALLER'S OPTIONAL QUESTIONS
===============================================================================
All are OFF unless you say yes. Anything your router doesn't have is skipped. Run the
installer again and choose 3 to undo all of them.

  HTTPS-only admin page
      Your login always travels encrypted. Your browser may warn once about the
      router's own certificate (click Advanced > Continue).
  Lock the VPN key file
      The file with your WireGuard private keys becomes readable only by the
      router's admin account. No downside. If you use OpenVPN, the files where GL
      saves your VPN provider login are kept root-only the same way (re-locked
      automatically after you save VPN settings).
  Close unused IPsec ports
      Two internet-facing firewall openings that are only needed for an IPsec VPN
      server. Say no if you run one.
  Kernel security settings
      Hides kernel memory addresses, blocks a feature (unprivileged BPF) often used in
      attacks, and ignores/stops ICMP redirects. No visible effect. (The BPF part only
      fully reverts after a reboot.)
  Check package signatures
      The router verifies GL's digital signature on add-on packages before installing
      them. Skipped automatically if it can't be verified.
  Remove an unused FTP entry
      A dormant config line on some firmware that would share the whole router over
      FTP if that service were ever started. No downside.
  Turn off DPI (app detection)
      Frees about 60 MB of memory. You lose per-app traffic statistics and app-based
      parental controls.
  Turn off cellular / SMS
      Only used with a USB 4G/5G modem or SIM.
  Turn off Bonjour / mDNS (avahi)
      Used to announce the router's own file sharing to Macs. Keep it if you share a
      USB drive from the router.
  Turn off the Bark client
      Only needed for the Bark parental-control service.
  VPN speed tuning (Brume 3 only)
      Spreads packet handling over 3 CPU cores and enlarges the packet queue. On a
      Brume 3 this removed packet drops and gave about 20% more combined VPN speed.
      Mostly matters at gigabit speeds.

Deliberately NOT offered: "SSH key-only login". It's more secure, but it would lock this
installer out, so it couldn't update or remove the Home tab later.

6. PRIVACY AND SECURITY
===============================================================================
  - The Home tab only works when you're logged into the router's admin page.
  - Privacy mode (eye icon): hides MAC addresses, public IP, gateway, device names and
    your town, so you can share screenshots safely. Hidden values are never even
    sent to the page, so nothing leaks in the page source.
  - Passwords (NAS, AdGuard) and the Discord webhook are stored on the router only,
    readable by its admin account only, and never shown again.
  - The installer never sees or saves your router password; you type it into
    Windows'/macOS's own SSH program.
  - Nothing is sent anywhere except:
      Discord        (if you add a webhook)
      Open-Meteo     (weather, if you set a ZIP)
      Iowa State     (radar map for the touchscreen, if that switch is on; the router
                      asks for a map of your area, about 4 MB a day)
      Ventusky       (only from YOUR browser, only while the radar window is open)
      Ookla          (speed tests, if you accepted)
      GL.iNet        (the router's normal firmware check)

8. FAQ / TROUBLESHOOTING
===============================================================================
Q: I don't see the Home tab / it looks old.
   Press Ctrl+F5 (Windows) or Cmd+Shift+R (Mac) on the admin page.

Q: Discord alerts don't arrive.
   Control Center > Settings > Discord alerts: check that alerts are switched on, then
   press Send test.
   The webhook must start with https://discord.com/api/webhooks/

Q: A card says "no data" or is empty.
   Wait about a minute after installing. Some cards only appear on models that have the
   hardware (fan, Wi-Fi, LEDs, touchscreen) or the feature (VPN tunnels, AdGuard).

Q: The speed test buttons don't work.
   You said no to Ookla's terms during install, or the download failed. Run the
   installer again (choose 1) and accept.

Q: My internet is slow at night.
   The nightly speed test briefly uses all your bandwidth (and about 15 GB of data per
   night when it also tests VPN tunnels). Change its time or turn it off in
   Control Center > Settings > Discord alerts > Thresholds & schedule.

Q: I got "Router settings changed" but I didn't change anything.
   Firmware updates, GL apps and the optional installer steps also change settings.
   The message lists exactly what changed.

Q: Will this void anything or break firmware upgrades?
   No stock GL.iNet files are modified; it uses GL's own plugin system, and everything
   can be removed with option 2. Firmware upgrades with "Keep settings" keep it.

Q: The installer says it can't reach the router.
   Make sure the computer is connected to that router and the IP is the one you use to
   open its admin page. SSH must be on (it is by default on GL.iNet routers).
